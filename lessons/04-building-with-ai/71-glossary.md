# Glossary — Plain English Definitions

> ⏱ 2 min read · 🟡 Intermediate

## One Line Answer
A glossary is a list of key terms with plain English definitions so you always know what technical words mean when you encounter them while building.

## Real World Analogy
It's like the index at the back of a textbook — you don't read it cover to cover, you flip to it when you hit a word that stops you cold.

## Live Example
Visit [MDN Web Docs Glossary](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Glossary) — every term like "API," "DOM," or "render" has a short plain-language definition at the top before the technical detail.

## How It Shows Up in a Real App

When someone on your team writes documentation or comments in code, shared definitions prevent confusion:

```html
<!-- "Component" = a reusable chunk of UI, like a button or nav bar -->
<!-- "Props" = settings you pass into a component to change how it looks -->
```

Internal glossaries live in team wikis, README files, or onboarding docs — anywhere a new person might get lost.

## What Breaks Without It
Without shared definitions, team members talk past each other — one person says "endpoint" meaning a URL, another thinks it means a page. Misread jargon is one of the most common reasons beginners get stuck or build the wrong thing.

## What to Tell AI When You Need It

> "I'm a beginner. Explain what [term] means in one sentence, then give me a real-world analogy."

> "List the 10 most important web development terms I need to know before building my first app. Keep each definition under 20 words."
